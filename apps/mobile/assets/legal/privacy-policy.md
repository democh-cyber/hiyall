## 5. Online status, last seen and Nearby

**Online and last seen.** You appear as online while the app is open or you were active in the last two minutes. When you are not online, others may see a "last seen" time that is rounded down to the nearest five minutes. One switch controls both: **Settings > Show when I'm online and last seen**. If you switch it off, others see neither. For under-18s it starts off. When it is hidden, it looks the same to other people as being offline.

**Nearby.**

- Nearby lists members close to you.
- When you allow the app to read your location, the app sends an approximate position to our server. Our server rounds it before saving it, to about 1 kilometre for adults and about 11 kilometres for under-18s. We do not save your exact coordinates.
- Other members never see coordinates. They see a distance in steps (for example "within 5 km"). For under-18s, the steps are wider (10, 25, 50 or 100 km).
- Your saved position counts as shared for 24 hours after the last update. It is deleted after 7 days if it is not refreshed. Signing out deletes it straight away.
- Only members in your age group can see you in Nearby. Members under 18 are never shown in adults' Nearby lists, and the other way round.
- There is currently no separate switch for Nearby inside Hiyall. The control is the location permission on your phone: if you turn it off in your phone's settings, the app stops sending your location, and your saved position expires as above.
- Rounding and distance steps reduce risk, but they are not perfect. A determined person who fakes locations from many places could narrow down where someone is to within a few kilometres. If this concerns you, turn off location for Hiyall.

## 6. Chats and end-to-end encryption

One-to-one chats, including photos, are end-to-end encrypted. Your phone encrypts a message before it leaves, and only the other person's phone can decrypt it. Our servers hold only scrambled data. We do not have the keys and cannot read your chats, so we cannot give them to anyone, including the authorities.

What this does not mean:

- **The server still sees information about chats.** For example: which accounts are chatting, when messages are sent, roughly how large they are, how many photos are sent and when, and which of your devices receive them. We also see the blocks and reports connected to a chat.
- **Your phone is the weak point.** Anyone who can unlock your phone, or the other person's phone, can read the chats stored there. Messages are kept on your device (the app keeps the most recent 500 per chat). Phone backups, if your phone's own backup feature includes them, are governed by Apple's or Google's terms, not ours.
- **The other person can share or screenshot it.** If someone reports a chat, they can choose to send us up to 10 messages and the photos in them. If you take part in a chat that is reported, those messages may be seen by our safety team. We only see what the reporter chooses to share.
- **Group chats and spaces are different.** Discussions in spaces are not end-to-end encrypted.
- **Safety numbers.** The app shows a safety number you can compare with the other person to check nobody has swapped keys. It does not do this for you automatically, and a mark that you verified it goes away if either of you adds or removes a device.
- **A new device on an account can read new messages.** Anyone who can sign in to an account, for example someone who learns its password, can add a device that reads the chats sent to that account from then on. It cannot read older messages, which stay on the phones that already had them. We do not yet check new devices cryptographically against the one you already trust. Instead the app tells you: your other devices show a banner, "A new device was added to your account", with a link to remove it, and the people you chat with see a notice in each conversation the next time they open it. If you see a device you do not recognise, remove it in Profile, Chat devices, and change your password.
- **Leaving the app.** Signing out or deleting your account erases your chats, keys and cached photos from that phone. On an iPhone, if you delete the app without signing out, what the system keeps is erased the next time you open Hiyall after reinstalling. Chat devices that go unused for 90 days are removed from our servers.
- Chats and chat photos can be used on the Android and iOS apps. They are not available in the web version.
